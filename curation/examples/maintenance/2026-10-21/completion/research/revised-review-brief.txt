Scope:A bounded immune-mediated podocytopathy map of primary minimal change nephropathy: anti-nephrin autoantibodies with humoral and Th2 associations, observational hemopexin activity, T-cell and cytokine (IL-8, IL-13) observations, experimental antibody and IL-13 models, foot-process effacement and nephrotic syndrome. Excludes secondary causes, genetics, other glomerulopathies and treatment efficacy; CD80, CTLA-4 and ANGPTL4 evidence is not yet curated.
Primary minimal change disease (MCD) is represented here as an immune-associated podocytopathy with heterogeneous upstream pathogenesis, foot-process effacement and nephrotic proteinuria. Anti-nephrin autoantibodies identify a subset: one multicentre cohort detected them in 44% of adults with MCD and 69% with active untreated disease. Levels tracked activity and proteinuria, fell during remission and reappeared before relapse; replication supports a subset but prevalence is quantitatively inconsistent. A cell-based assay cohort detected antibodies in 7/36 adults with MCD (19.44%); another pediatric/young-adult cohort found 15% MCD and 11% FSGS positivity. Anti-nephrin is neither universal nor specific to MCD. Human biopsy findings associate antibodies with podocyte IgG. Nephrin immunization in mice induced an MCD-like syndrome, nephrin phosphorylation and podocyte cytoskeletal changes. Patient IgG transferred to a rabbit caused proteinuria, effacement without deposits and nephrin phosphorylation. These experiments support a pathogenic antibody route in selected systems, without establishing each intracellular step or explaining antibody-negative disease. Anti-nephrin-positive patients also had IgG4 predominance, Th2-skewed cytokines and a non-significant plasmablast trend; their sequence is unresolved. A newly added human study found higher hemopexin protease activity despite lower hemopexin concentration during MCD relapse, compared with remission. This supplies a separate observational circulating-factor signal, not proof that hemopexin causes human MCD.

The antibody-transfer experiment used total IgG from one patient in one rabbit, rather than purified nephrin-specific antibody; animal sufficiency does not establish universal human necessity. The hemopexin study compared 18 relapsing patients, 23 patients in remission after prednisolone, other glomerulopathies and controls; treatment and disease-stage confounding remain. Its kidney-tissue activity assay did not establish in vivo permeability-factor causation. Peripheral CD4 activation at onset remains an association. IL-13 overexpression in rats caused albuminuria, foot-process fusion and changes in nephrin and B7-1 expression, supporting an overexpression-model route. IL-13 effects remain model-dependent; this does not establish uniform pathogenicity or human necessity. Urinary IL-8 rose in relapse, yet that study did not support a disease-specific pathogenic role for IL-8 or TNF-alpha under its tested conditions. No single upstream factor is established as necessary or sufficient across primary MCD. Concurrent steroid-induced remission and antibody clearance do not isolate the mediator of treatment response. Secondary causes, other glomerulopathies and treatment efficacy are excluded from this synthesis. Human observations and animal experiments retain separate graph contexts; most sources, including the newly added hemopexin report, were inspected at abstract level. Independent domain review and calibrated appraisals remain pending.

Scope:Bounded primary (autoantibody-mediated) membranous nephropathy map: podocyte target antigens (PLA2R1, THSD7A and other recently described antigens) lumped as one shared antigen-antibody mechanism, subepithelial immune-complex formation, complement activation, podocyte injury and nephrotic proteinuria. Secondary membranous nephropathy (lupus, malignancy, infection, drug-associated) and therapy efficacy are excluded.
Primary membranous nephropathy shares an antigen-specific podocyte autoantibody principle, but antigen-defined subtypes differ in frequency, serology and clinical associations and are increasingly treated as partly distinct entities. Autoantibodies, IgG4-predominant though not IgG4-exclusive, bind a podocyte-associated antigen, not all of which are cell-surface proteins: PLA2R in most inspected adult cases, THSD7A in a smaller PLA2R-negative subgroup, NELL-1 in selected PLA2R-negative cases, and semaphorin 3B, HTRA1 or rarer putative targets beyond those. The evidence differs in kind by antigen: deposit-eluted IgG recognized PLA2R and THSD7A, patient sera recognized NELL-1, semaphorin 3B and HTRA1, and the seven putative mass-spectrometry targets were recovered from immune complexes and co-localized with IgG in the deposits without comparable serological evidence. Anti-PLA2R levels tracked clinical status and proteinuria, and epitope spreading beyond the cysteine-rich domain was an independent risk factor for poor renal outcome. HLA-DQA1 and PLA2R1 risk loci were associated with biopsy-proven disease in white-ancestry cohorts.

Antigen-specific rodent models supply directional evidence: podocyte expression of human PLA2R1 produced antibodies, deposits and nephrotic syndrome, lymphocyte-deficient animals developed neither, and Thsd7a-knockout mice resisted antibody transfer. These do not establish one human effector route. Patient-biopsy assays detected all three complement initiation pathways, with proximity assays favouring the classical pathway; factor-B-null mice were protected and lectin-pathway blockade preserved slit proteins in culture. A newly added retrospective comparison of 17 PLA2R-associated and nine NELL1-associated cases found lower C3 and C4d deposition in NELL1 disease. Deposition is not functional activation, proteomic complement differences were not significant, and this study did not test slit-diaphragm injury. Effector evidence also differs by system: C5b-9 was required for slit-protein proteolysis in culture and C5 deficiency partly reduced albuminuria, while membrane-attack-complex inhibition did not restore albumin permselectivity on a glomerulus-on-a-chip and anti-THSD7A antibodies injured podocytes in C3-deficient mice. These non-exclusive pathways differ by subtype, assay and experimental system; none is established as universal. The under-1-to-4.9% frequencies concern seven putative targets, not NELL-1. In a separate 70-person NELL1 series only 11% lacked known secondary associations, including drugs, autoimmunity or malignancy. NELL1 identity does not establish primary disease; its inclusion is bounded and provisional against mixed literature, with secondary associations excluded. Evidence rests on agent-inspected abstracts; therapy efficacy and independent domain review remain outside the completed work.
