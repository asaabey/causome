anca-associated-vasculitis: Bounded MPO-/PR3-ANCA, neutrophil activation, complement/C5a and renal/pulmonary small-vessel injury map for GPA/MPA, with a separate EGPA eosinophilic branch. Human observations and experimental models remain distinct; excludes drug-induced disease and comprehensive diagnostic or therapy coverage.
anti-gbm-disease: Bounded anti-GBM autoimmunity to alpha3/alpha5 type IV collagen NC1 epitopes, HLA-restricted T-cell responses, antibody-mediated renal and pulmonary injury; human observations and experimental models remain distinct. Excludes Alport post-transplant nephritis and atypical seronegative disease.
atypical-hus: An integrated aHUS map connecting inherited and acquired complement dysregulation, endothelial and platelet injury, microvascular thrombosis, haemolysis, thrombocytopenia and kidney dysfunction. Includes heme amplification and C5 targets; study-specific limits and disputed THBD genetics remain explicit.
behcet-disease: Bounded human Behçet disease overview: HLA/ERAP1 and cytokine-locus susceptibility, T-cell and neutrophil activation, NET/coagulation and endothelial injury, plus mucocutaneous, ocular, vascular, neurologic, joint and intestinal branches. Primary-report associations, culture mechanisms and intervention populations remain distinct. Excludes monogenic mimics, diagnostic criteria and comprehensive treatment guidance.
c3-glomerulonephritis: Bounded C3GN map: acquired convertase dysregulation, complement-regulatory and CFHR structural variants, glomerular complement deposition and variable renal course. Related mixed-C3G assay/cohort evidence and mixed-C3G/IC-MPGN trials retain separate contexts. Dense deposit disease and immune-complex GN are excluded as mapped phenotypes; their inclusion in cited studies is explicit. Selected proximal complement therapies include verified US indications through 2026-10-15; no universal causal circuit or comprehensive therapy catalog is claimed.
calciphylaxis: A bounded map of calcific uraemic arteriolopathy: mineral-metabolism drivers, calcification-inhibitor deficiency (MGP/vitamin K, fetuin-A), vascular smooth-muscle osteogenic change, arteriolar calcification, thrombosis and skin ischaemic necrosis, plus curated therapies.
crest-syndrome: A bounded limited cutaneous systemic sclerosis map: anticentromere autoimmunity, endothelial vasculopathy with a pulmonary arterial hypertension branch, fibroblast activation and calcinosis associations. Raynaud's phenomenon has no supported edge yet. Diffuse cutaneous SSc-specific features, scleroderma renal crisis, morphea and overlap syndromes are out of scope.
diffuse-systemic-sclerosis: Diffuse cutaneous systemic sclerosis: autoimmunity, endothelial injury, fibroblast activation and skin fibrosis, with interstitial lung disease and renal crisis branches. Mixed-subset SSc studies retain their context; excludes limited cutaneous disease, morphea and overlap syndromes.
iga-nephropathy: IgA1 glycosylation, antiglycan antibodies and mesangial signalling, with study-specific limits.
igg4-related-disease: Bounded cross-organ immune-mechanism map of IgG4-related disease: B cells/plasmablasts, T-cell responses and fibrosis-related findings. Selected human primary reports, not comprehensive organ-specific coverage; excludes mimics, diagnostic criteria, epidemiology and treatment efficacy.
membranous-nephropathy: Bounded primary (autoantibody-mediated) membranous nephropathy map: podocyte target antigens (PLA2R1, THSD7A and other recently described antigens) lumped as one shared antigen-antibody mechanism, subepithelial immune-complex formation, complement activation, podocyte injury and nephrotic proteinuria. Secondary membranous nephropathy (lupus, malignancy, infection, drug-associated) and therapy efficacy are excluded.
minimal-change-nephropathy: A bounded podocyte-injury circuit of primary minimal change nephropathy: circulating factors and T-cell activity, foot-process effacement and nephrotic syndrome. Excludes secondary causes and other glomerulopathies.
pre-eclampsia: A bounded pre-eclampsia map: defective placentation and placental ischaemia/oxidative stress, the antiangiogenic sFlt-1/soluble endoglin/PlGF axis, maternal endothelial dysfunction with renal, hepatic and cerebral end-organ outcomes, and immune/complement/AT1 autoantibody contributions. Chronic hypertension without pre-eclampsia, gestational hypertension alone, postpartum-onset disease, screening performance and therapy efficacy are out of scope.
primary-fsgs: Bounded circulating-factor/immune primary FSGS and separate APOL1-associated kidney injury branch; excludes other genetic, adaptive, viral and drug-associated FSGS. APOL1-associated disease is not classified as idiopathic primary FSGS.
sjogren-disease: Bounded primary Sjögren disease: epithelial activation, interferon, BAFF/B-cell activity, autoantibodies, ectopic lymphoid organisation and glandular dysfunction; excludes secondary disease, mimics and symptomatic therapy efficacy.
sle: A bounded nucleic-acid, interferon and B-cell circuit with a renal injury branch.

Exact full-text PPi RESULTS (PMC13571338, DOI10.1016/j.jacadv.2026.103205):
At enrollment, for the entire calciphylaxis cohort (n = 70), the median age was 60 years (IQR: 53-70). Cohort was predominantly comprised of females (69%) and Caucasian race (71%). Comorbidities included ESKD (67%), diabetes (78%), coronary artery disease (44%) with a median body mass index (BMI) of 31 kg/m2 (IQR: 27-37), and 37% on warfarin. Serum calcium and phosphorous were mostly unremarkable. The median intact parathyroid hormone and alkaline phosphatase (ALP) were elevated (median intact parathyroid hormone: 130 pg/mL, IQR: 42-252; median ALP: 123 IU/L, IQR: 89-192) compared to reference ranges. The median skin lesion count was 3 (IQR: 2-4) and median pain intensity was 5 (IQR: 4-7) on a 0 to 10 scale. Mortality was 21% (15 dead, 55 alive) by 6-week and 27% (19 dead, 51 alive) by 12-week follow-up. Deaths were caused by either sepsis originating from wound infection or withdrawal from dialysis due to disease burden.
For case-control analysis, patient characteristics were well-balanced between cases (n = 30) and matched controls (n = 30), except for serum ALP which was higher in cases (median: 132 IU/L; IQR: 102-214) than in controls (median: 99 IU/L; IQR: 75-147, P = 0.03). ESKD was present in 67% of cases and controls (Table 1). PPi levels were significantly lower among patients with calciphylaxis compared to matched controls, (cases: median: 248 nM, [IQR: 110-323]; controls: median: 611 nM, [IQR: 361-799]; P < 0.001). The finding of lower PPi levels in calciphylaxis compared to controls was consistent in analyses stratified by presence or absence of ESKD. Moreover, PPi levels in patients with calciphylaxis were substantially lower than those previously reported for healthy adults by Khursigara et al5 (median: 2,167 nM [IQR: 1,787-2,969]). In conditional logistic regression model adjusted for ALP, phosphate, and BMI, each 100 nM reduction in PPi was associated with a 68% higher odds of calciphylaxis (OR: 1.68, 95% CI: 1.26-2.42).
At enrollment, PPi levels negatively correlated with the number of skin lesions (rs = −0.26; P = 0.03) but not with pain intensity. Compared to calciphylaxis patients who were alive by 6 weeks, those who died had significantly lower enrollment PPi levels (245 nM [IQR: 78-543] vs 727 nM [IQR: 341-1,232], P = 0.002). Among ESKD patients with calciphylaxis, each 100 nM reduction in enrollment PPi was associated with ∼50% higher 6-week mortality after adjusting for age, sex, and BMI (OR: 1.54; 95% CI: 1.09-2.18). A similar association was also noted between enrollment PPi and 12-week mortality. There was a substantial within-individual variability between 6-week and enrollment PPi levels.
