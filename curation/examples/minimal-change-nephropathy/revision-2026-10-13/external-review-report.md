## 1. Overall assessment

The candidate is scientifically cautious and does not make major false causal assertions. However, it is substantially incomplete as an overall mechanistic explanation of primary minimal change disease (MCD).

Its principal weakness is not that the three selected studies are necessarily incorrect. It is that they disproportionately represent older immunological observations, cytokine associations and an individual clinical case, while omitting the most important recent advance in MCD pathogenesis: anti-nephrin autoantibody-associated podocyte injury.

By 2024–2026, evidence had advanced considerably beyond the traditional hypothesis of T-cell dysregulation releasing unidentified permeability factors. Human studies have identified circulating anti-nephrin antibodies in a substantial subset of patients with active MCD, and experimental studies support their pathogenic potential.

The candidate also lacks an adequate description of how the proposed immune abnormalities converge on the glomerular filtration barrier to produce foot-process effacement and nephrotic-range proteinuria.

Recommendation: Revise the summary around immune-mediated podocyte injury, distinguish the emerging antibody-mediated mechanism from older proposed cytokine pathways, and preserve the possibility of mechanistic heterogeneity. This can be accomplished within the existing scope. A wider disease atlas or inclusion of secondary MCD is not required.

## 3. Evidence supporting the revisions

### A. Anti-nephrin antibodies are the most important missing development

The pivotal 2024 multicentre study by Hengel and colleagues examined 539 patients and 117 controls: 44% of adults with MCD anti-nephrin positive overall (46/105); 69% in active, untreated adult MCD; 90% in active, untreated childhood idiopathic nephrotic syndrome. These are cohort- and assay-dependent figures, not universal prevalences. Antibody concentrations correlated with clinical disease activity. Immunisation with nephrin produced MCD-like podocyte injury and nephrotic syndrome in mice.

Hengel et al. (2025) demonstrated that passive transfer of patient-derived anti-nephrin antibodies could produce a podocytopathy with minimal-change lesions in an experimental animal model. That strengthens the argument beyond correlation, but experimental induction of a phenotype does not prove that the same mechanism explains every human case.

Suggested correction: Represent anti-nephrin-mediated podocytopathy as a strongly supported pathogenic mechanism in a subset of primary MCD, while leaving its prevalence, initiating immunological events and contribution to antibody-negative disease unresolved.

Sources: https://doi.org/10.1056/NEJMoa2314471 ; https://doi.org/10.1172/JCI186769 ; https://doi.org/10.1097/MNH.0000000000001167

### B. The podocyte pathway must be explicit

Proposed bounded causal architecture: immune dysregulation (upstream initiating defects incompletely defined) → anti-nephrin autoantibodies (supported pathogenic pathway in a subset) and/or T-cell/cytokine pathways (proposed, uncertain human causality) → podocyte dysfunction (slit-diaphragm signalling and cytoskeletal disruption) → foot-process effacement and altered filtration-barrier permeability → heavy albuminuria and nephrotic syndrome. The two upstream branches are alternative or potentially interacting mechanisms, not two independently established pathways operating in every patient. The exact intracellular sequence connecting antibody binding to endocytosis, phosphorylation changes and cytoskeletal reorganisation remains under investigation.

### C. T-cell activation remains relevant but is not a proven master dysregulation

Expansion of activated peripheral CD4 T cells and increased CD69 expression support immune involvement but do not establish that T cells originate the disease, produce the causal permeability factor or directly injure podocytes. IL-13 overexpression can produce MCD-like proteinuria in rats (experimental sufficiency, not human necessity). Humoral and T-cell explanations need not be mutually exclusive. The 2026 steroid study: 12 of 17 treatment-naïve adults antibody-positive; antibody disappearance accompanied remission after eight weeks of corticosteroids — supportive longitudinal evidence, not proof that antibody reduction alone explains therapeutic benefit.

Sources: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8733331/ ; https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ekir.2026.103794

### D. Additional proposed factors should not be promoted to established causes

CD80/B7-1, CTLA-4, ANGPTL4 and other candidate mediators are reasonable exploratory/contested mechanisms; human evidence is inconsistent (CD80 staining varies; urinary ANGPTL4 elevation not MCD-specific).

## 5. Corrected two-paragraph scientific summary (proposed replacement)

Primary minimal change disease (MCD) is an immune-associated podocytopathy characterised by disruption of glomerular filtration-barrier function, diffuse podocyte foot-process effacement and nephrotic-range proteinuria. Its upstream pathogenesis is heterogeneous and incompletely resolved. An increasingly well-supported mechanism involves circulating autoantibodies against nephrin, a critical structural and signalling component of the podocyte slit diaphragm. Anti-nephrin antibodies are detectable in a substantial, assay-dependent subset of patients with active MCD, correlate with disease activity, and have produced MCD-like podocyte injury and nephrotic syndrome in experimental animal models, including passive-transfer studies using patient-derived antibodies. These findings support a pathogenic antibody-mediated mechanism in some patients, potentially involving altered nephrin signalling, slit-diaphragm integrity and podocyte actin-cytoskeletal organisation. The initiating immune abnormalities, precise downstream molecular sequence and mechanisms operating in antibody-negative disease remain unresolved.

T-cell activation and Th2-associated immune responses have also been reported in human MCD, including increased peripheral CD4 T-cell activation and altered cytokine or chemokine expression. However, these findings are primarily associative and have not established a universal T-cell-derived permeability factor. Candidate mediators including IL-13, IL-8, TNF-α, CD80 and ANGPTL4 have varying experimental support, but human evidence of disease-specific causality remains incomplete or conflicting. Multiple immune pathways may therefore converge on podocyte dysfunction, foot-process effacement and increased albumin permeability. Spontaneous remission and relapse illustrate clinical variability without independently identifying the initiating mechanism. No single upstream immune defect has been established as necessary or sufficient for all cases of primary MCD.

## 7. Final disposition

Needs revision. Required before reconsideration: incorporate the anti-nephrin pathway, describe the podocyte injury mechanism, and rebalance the evidence away from T-cell associations and individual cases. No scope expansion required. Residual uncertainty: antibody-negative disease, T-cell–B-cell interactions, other circulating factors and the exact intracellular signalling sequence. This review does not constitute publication approval or exhaustive confirmation of the underlying disease map.
